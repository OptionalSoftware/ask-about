## What the firm does

Fennwick Harbor Engineering designs, assesses, and oversees the construction
of structures that meet water: quay walls, jetties, breakwaters, lock gates,
movable and fixed bridges, and the flood defences around them. It works for
port authorities, transport agencies, utilities, and the contractors building
for them. [public]

Services fall into four lines:

- **Design.** Concept through detailed design, including structural,
  geotechnical, and hydraulic engineering, and the drawings and
  specifications a contractor builds from.
- **Assessment.** Condition surveys, load-rating, and remaining-life studies
  of existing structures, including underwater inspection by the firm's own
  dive team.
- **Construction supervision.** Resident engineers on site, inspection and
  test witnessing, and sign-off against the design.
- **Advisory.** Independent checking of other firms' designs, expert-witness
  work, and asset-management strategy for owners with large estates of ageing
  structures.

It does not do dredging, environmental impact assessment, or architecture.
It partners with firms that do, and names them in proposals when a project
needs them.

## Who it works for [public]

- Port and harbour authorities, from single-berth fishing harbours to
  container terminals.
- National and regional transport agencies responsible for road and rail
  bridges over water.
- Water utilities and flood authorities.
- Marine contractors, as their designer or as an independent checker.

Clients are mostly in the United Kingdom, Ireland, and the Netherlands, with
occasional work in the Baltic states and West Africa through partner firms.

  bridge. Still in daily use; the firm carries out its five-yearly principal
  inspection.

  Delivered with no reportable safety incidents over 31 months of

## How the firm works

### Engagement

Work comes in three ways: framework agreements with long-term clients (about
55% of revenue), competitive tenders (about 30%), and direct appointment
(about 15%). The firm does not respond to tenders where the scope is
dredging-led or where it would be a subcontractor to a design-and-build
contractor without an independent line to the client.

A first conversation is with a director. A proposal names the project team,
the deliverables, the programme, and a fee — fixed price for defined scope,
time-charge for assessment and advisory work where the scope depends on what
is found.

### Quality

Every design is checked by an engineer who did not produce it, and every
design for a structure carrying the public is checked by a second team under
the Technical Director. The quality system is certified to ISO 9001; the
health and safety system to ISO 45001. Both certifications have been held
since 2009 and 2020 respectively.

### Fees

Hourly rates by grade are published to framework clients and reviewed each
April. The firm does not publish rates on its website. Fixed-price design
fees are typically 4–8% of construction value depending on complexity, with
movable structures at the top of that range.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do you work outside the UK, Ireland, and the Netherlands?**
Occasionally, through partner firms, in the Baltic states and West Africa.
The firm does not open offices for single projects.

**Can you assess a structure you didn't design?**
Yes; that is most of the assessment work. Original drawings help but are not
required — the dive team and survey work can establish what is there.

**Do you do environmental assessments?**
No. The firm partners with environmental consultancies and names them in
proposals when a project needs one.

**Who owns the firm?**
An employee ownership trust holding 100% of the shares since 2019. There are
no external investors.

**How do I get in touch about a project?**
Through the enquiry form on the website, which reaches a director in the
nearest office within two working days.
