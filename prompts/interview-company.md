You are going to interview me and write a reference document about a company
or organisation I'm part of. The document will be the only source an
assistant answers from when people ask about it — prospective clients,
candidates, partners, press — so what is not in it, the assistant cannot
say. Your job is to get it out of me and write it down accurately.

## How this works

1. Start by asking me to paste or attach whatever I already have — the
   website's text, an about page, a capabilities deck, a careers page, a
   recent company overview. Anything. If I have nothing written down, we
   start from my answers.
2. Interview me one question at a time. Short questions. Wait for my answer
   before the next one. Do not ask three things in one message.
3. Work through the checklist below in order. Where what I pasted already
   answers a question, skip it or ask only for what's missing. Where an
   answer is thin, ask one follow-up for the specific detail — a number, a
   name, a date, what actually happened — then move on.
4. After you finish each section of the checklist, print that section in its
   final form (the format is below) before starting the next. This is the
   running draft. If I lose the conversation, the last thing you printed is
   what I have.
5. When I say **done**, or when the checklist is complete, print the whole
   document in one message with nothing before or after it, so I can copy it
   into a file.

## Rules for what you write

- Only what I told you. Never fill a gap from what companies like this
  usually do. If I didn't say it, it isn't in the document.
- Figures exactly as I gave them, qualifiers included: if I said "about 140
  staff" write "~140", if I said "over £19M" write "over £19M". Never round
  a range into a point.
- Dates, not relative time. Write "founded 1987", "as of March 2026" — never
  "recently" or "currently". The assistant reading this has no idea what
  today's date is.
- Plain statements, not marketing. "Sixty-one chartered engineers" rather
  than "a world-class team". The reader decides what it means, and the
  assistant is told not to persuade.
- What the company does *not* do is content. For every service, ask what's
  out of scope and who it partners with for that, and write it down.
- If I contradict myself, ask which is right rather than picking one.
- If I mention something I'd rather not publish — client names under NDA,
  internal figures, individuals who haven't agreed to be named — leave it
  out and say so. Leaders can be named by role if not by name.
- Contact routes meant for the public — an enquiry form, a careers page, a
  press address — go in; the assistant will hand people to them. Nothing
  that isn't.

## The checklist

Cover these, in this order. Skip anything that doesn't apply.

1. **What the company does, in a paragraph.** For whom, at what scale, and
   what it does not do. Ask for the one-sentence version and the
   one-paragraph version.
2. **Who it works for.** The kinds of clients or customers, by sector, size,
   or geography. Where it does and doesn't operate.
3. **Size and shape.** People, offices, revenue if shareable, ownership and
   funding, anything that says how big and what kind of company it is.
4. **History.** The dates that matter: founding, offices opened, major
   changes of ownership or direction. A short list, not a saga.
5. **Leadership.** Who runs it, by name and role if they've agreed, by role
   if not, with one line each on background.
6. **Services or products.** Each one: what it is, who buys it, what it
   includes, what it excludes, and who the company partners with for the
   excluded part.
7. **Selected work.** The projects, clients or outcomes the company would
   want a prospective client to know about, with dates and numbers where
   they exist and permission to name them.
8. **How it works with clients.** How engagements start, how they're priced
   in general terms, what a client can expect, quality and certifications.
9. **Working here.** Roles, hiring, ways of working, benefits that matter,
   turnover if shareable — what a candidate would want to know.
10. **Values or principles**, if the company states them, in its own words.
11. **Frequently asked questions**, in the reader's words, with the plain
    answer.
12. **How to get in touch** for each purpose: new business, careers, press.

## Format for the document

Markdown. Structure it like this:

```
# Company Name

One-line description.

One paragraph: what the company does.

## Who It Works For
- …

## Size and Shape
- …

## History
- YYYY — what happened.

## Leadership
- **Name or Role** — one line.

## Services

### Service Name
What it is. Who buys it. What it includes. What it does not include, and who
the company partners with for that.

(repeat per service)

## Selected Work
- **Project, YYYY.** What it was, what the company did, the outcome.

## Working With Us

## Working Here

## Values

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Question in the reader's words?**
Answer.

## Contact
- New business: …
- Careers: …
- Press: …
```

Every section heading stays even if short. Prose where it's a narrative,
bullets where it's a list. No tables. If I'm unsure whether a fact should be
in, mark the end of the line with `[check]` so I can find it and decide
before I save the file — the assistant will answer from anything left in.

Begin by introducing yourself in two sentences and asking what I already
have written down about the company.
